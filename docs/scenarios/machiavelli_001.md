## Segment 1 — The Loudest Man
prompt: dark renaissance palace interior, solitary figure in black cloak standing in shadow, chiaroscuro lighting, old master oil painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Most people mistake power for noise. They think the leader is the loudest voice in the room, the one who never stops talking, whose name is spoken most often. Machiavelli would call this a mistake. Not a moral mistake — a strategic one."
subtitle: Power is not in loudness.

## Segment 2 — Step 1: Become Impossible to Ignore
prompt: renaissance nobleman in dark clothing, looking at the viewer with calm intensity, candlelight, old master portrait, umber and gold palette
voice: "Step one. Become impossible to ignore. Machiavelli writes that a prince must be loved, or feared, but never hated. The quiet man who is loved by those he serves is more dangerous than the loud man who is merely tolerated. People follow the man they trust with their lives, not the man who shouts the loudest."
subtitle: Step 1 — Become impossible to ignore.

## Segment 3 — Step 2: Speak Once, Strike Once
prompt: renaissance military commander on horseback, sword drawn, dramatic sky, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Step two. Speak once, strike once. Machiavelli admired Cesare Borgia. Borgia did not argue for hours. He sent a message once, clearly, and then he acted. When you are quiet, every word you say carries more weight. The room learns to listen."
subtitle: Step 2 — Speak once, strike once.

## Segment 4 — Step 3: Let the Room Guess
prompt: shadowy figure in a renaissance chamber, half-lit face, candlelight, mysterious atmosphere, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Step three. Let the room guess. An exposed intention is a weak one. Machiavelli wrote that men judge more by what they see than by what they hear. If you hide your hand, your opponent cannot prepare. Silence is not weakness. It is withheld information."
subtitle: Step 3 — Let the room guess.

## Segment 5 — Step 4: Become the Frame, Not the Painting
prompt: renaissance throne room, empty throne with crown on it, dramatic lighting, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Step four. Become the frame, not the painting. The loudest man wants to be the painting — beautiful, visible, praised. The powerful man becomes the frame — the thing that holds the picture together. When you are the frame, the painting cannot exist without you."
subtitle: Step 4 — Become the frame, not the painting.

## Segment 6 — Step 5: End with a Door Open
prompt: open doorway in a renaissance palace, light streaming through, mysterious, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Step five. Always end with a door open. Machiavelli warned that a prince who is too open invites betrayal. But a prince who leaves one door unlocked, one possibility alive, keeps every man guessing which door he will walk through. That is quiet power."
subtitle: Step 5 — End with a door open.

## Segment 7 — What Machiavelli Never Wrote
prompt: old leather book on a desk, quill pen, inkwell, candlelight, renaissance study, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Machiavelli never wrote this as five steps. He wrote it as warnings and examples. But read the Prince with these five moves in mind, and you will see them everywhere — in condottieri, in popes, in merchants who became kings. The loud man is remembered. The quiet man is followed."
subtitle: The loud man is remembered. The quiet man is followed.

## Segment 8 — A Closing Thought
prompt: solitary figure walking away into fog, renaissance cityscape, moody atmosphere, old master painting, umber and gold palette
voice: "Power is not in loudness. It never was. It is in the space between words, in the silence before the decision, in the man who leaves the room and leaves behind exactly what he wanted. If this changed how you see the room you sit in, you already understand it."
subtitle: Power is not in loudness.
